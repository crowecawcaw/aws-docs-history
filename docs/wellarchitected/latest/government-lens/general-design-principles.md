# General design principles

This Government Lens lists high level design principles that
government customers expect to be applied to services being
developed with and for them. A version of these principles is found
in most governments today and in some jurisdictions. Services can be
audited to verify that they comply with service standards. Each
principle has one or more questions to consider that support the
pillar assessment. The principles outlined in this whitepaper draw
from
the [Australian
Digital Service Standard](https://www.dta.gov.au/help-and-advice/about-digital-service-standard "https://www.dta.gov.au/help-and-advice/about-digital-service-standard"), the
[Canadian
Digital Standards](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/government-canada-digital-standards.html "https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/government-canada-digital-standards.html"), the
[New
Zealand Digital Service Standard](https://www.digital.govt.nz/standards-and-guidance/digital-service-design-standard/ "https://www.digital.govt.nz/standards-and-guidance/digital-service-design-standard/"),
the [UK
Government Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard "https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard"), and the
[US Government Digital
Services Playbook](https://playbook.cio.gov/ "https://playbook.cio.gov/"). It’s important that builders are aware of
the standards for their jurisdiction. It is also worth being aware
of
the [Digital
Nations Charter](https://www.leadingdigitalgovs.org/ "https://www.leadingdigitalgovs.org/"), first signed in 2014 by the Digital Nations
cohort, including leading digital government from around the world.
