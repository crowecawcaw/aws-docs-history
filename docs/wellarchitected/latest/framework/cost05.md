

# Evaluate cost when selecting services

 Evaluating cost when selecting services means choosing each service and component on its full cost and fit. Total cost of ownership includes operational overhead, licensing, data transfer, and the effort to manage and maintain a component. A comparison based on listed price alone misleads. This capability covers the practices that make service selection a deliberate, cost-aware decision. 

 Good selection is comprehensive, data-driven, and proportional. It weighs every component and its dependencies, rests on actual and projected usage, and matches the effort of each analysis to the savings it can produce. It is also collaborative and continuous where technical, financial, and business stakeholders share the trade-offs, and choices are reassessed as workloads and available services change.

## Capability intent
<a name="cost05-intent"></a>
+  Service and component choices are evaluated against total cost of ownership, including operational and licensing overhead, rather than headline price alone. 
+  Business priorities and the trade-offs between cost, performance, reliability, and delivery speed are documented and agreed across product, engineering, finance, and business stakeholders. 
+  Every workload component is analyzed, with the depth of each review scaled to the savings it can realistically produce. 
+  Licensing is chosen so cost scales with usage or business outcome, and open-source options are considered for standard functions. 
+  Architecture favors managed, serverless, and event-driven designs where they lower cost while meeting the business value each component supports. 
+  Cost analysis runs continuously through forecasting, monitoring, and scheduled reassessment, so choices track changing usage and new service options. 

## Common issues to watch for
<a name="cost05-issues"></a>
+  Services are chosen for the lowest headline price, so operational overhead, licensing, and data transfer costs appear later and dwarf the original saving. 
+  Only the largest line items are analyzed, leaving a long tail of small components to accumulate into meaningful spend that no one is watching. 
+  Component choices are made for current volume alone, so a service that suits an early stage becomes a cost driver once usage grows and switching later carries migration effort. 
+  Comparisons count only infrastructure price and omit the staff time to patch, scale, and operate a component, so self-managed options look cheaper than the managed alternatives they lose to on total cost. 
+  Licenses tied to fixed attributes such as processor cores are accepted without question, so cost climbs with capacity even when that capacity sits idle. 
+  Service selection is treated as a launch-day decision and never revisited, so workloads keep running on costlier options after cheaper ones become available.
