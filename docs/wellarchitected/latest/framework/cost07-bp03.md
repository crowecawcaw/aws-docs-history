

# COST07-BP03 Select third-party agreements with cost-efficient terms

 Choose agreements whose cost scales with the benefit they deliver, keeping cost justified as your usage grows. 

 **Desired outcome:** 
+  Your third-party product and license costs scale in proportion to the value they deliver. 
+  Each of your agreements is justified by benefits that exceed its cost at current and projected usage. 
+  You consolidate overlapping tools so you do not pay twice for the same capability. 

 **Common anti-patterns:** 
+  Adopting third-party tools for capabilities that existing or native tools already provide. 
+  Paying license fees that exceed the value the product returns. 
+  Accepting pricing that scales with total spend even where the product benefits only part of it. 
+  Signing agreements without modeling cost at projected future usage. 

 **Benefits of establishing this best practice:** 
+  Spend on third-party products tracks the value those products return. 
+  Duplicate tooling costs fall as overlapping capabilities are consolidated. 
+  Agreements chosen with usage modeling avoid cost surprises as the workload grows. 

 **Level of risk exposed if this best practice is not established:** Medium 

## Implementation guidance
<a name="implementation-guidance"></a>

 Many products help you manage cloud costs, and they differ in features and pricing structure. Some focus on cost governance or visibility, others on optimization. The pricing model matters as much as feature fit, because it determines whether cost stays proportional to value as your usage changes. 

 Common pricing models align incentives differently. A percentage-of-savings model charges more as it saves more, tying cost to outcome. A percentage-of-total-bill model charges against spend the product may not influence, so it bills you for services that receive no benefit. A managed service model priced as a share of managed-resource cost can charge more for a larger instance that needs no extra management. 

 A recommendation tool limited to Amazon EC2 but priced as a percentage of your entire bill grows more expensive as you adopt other services. Before signing, confirm the pricing includes cost optimization features or a program that drives efficiency. 

 Weigh the product against what you already own and against your expected usage over the life of the agreement. A tool that looks economical at current scale can become the larger line item on the bill once the workload grows. 

### Implementation steps
<a name="implementation-steps"></a>

1.  **Model agreement costs at current and projected usage:** Review the pricing terms in each third-party agreement, then model cost across several usage levels. Factor in new service adoption and increases in existing services as the workload grows. Decide whether the additional cost delivers benefits your business requires. 

1.  **Check for overlap with existing tools:** Review native and already-owned tools to determine whether they provide the capability before you buy another. Decide whether an additional tool's benefit outweighs its added cost and the cost of running two tools that overlap. 

1.  **Confirm pricing scales with value:** Verify that the pricing model tracks the outcome the product delivers, not just your total spend or resource size. Prefer terms where the fee rises only when the benefit rises.
